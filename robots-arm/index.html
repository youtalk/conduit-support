    Teleoperate a robot arm

    While you hold HOLD, the arm's end effector follows the motion of your iPhone or iPad: Conduit turns ARKit's pose of the device into geometry_msgs/TwistStamped for MoveIt Servo (/servo_node/delta_twist_cmds by default). A gripper arc works the gripper, and a pose menu sends the arm to a named pose through MoveIt. On a Mac the arm view is view-only.

    Host setup

    Arms need MoveIt Servo from Jazzy or later: the arm profile reads moveit_msgs/ServoStatus and the overlay uses the moveit_servo. parameter namespace, which Humble's Servo publishes and configures differently.

    1. Servo overlay. Load conduit_teleop's Servo overlay after your robot's own Servo parameters, so Servo takes speeds and keeps twists for 0.5 s. The Servo overlay section below shows the file and how to load it.

    2. Time sync. Servo drops a twist whose stamp is older than incoming_command_timeout on its own clock, and it keeps moving on the last twist until then. Conduit therefore stamps every twist, moving or zero, on the robot's clock instead of the device's. It measures that clock from the profile's Joint states topic: the difference between each message's header.stamp and the time it arrives, taking the lowest-latency sample over the last 10 s, and it never stamps a twist ahead of the robot's real time. The 0.5 s stop when the link is lost then holds even when the device's clock differs from the host's, so neither chrony nor Set Automatically is needed for it. The requirements on the Joint states topic:

      • It must be stamped by the computer that runs Servo (the same clock Servo uses), and never in the future. Joint states published by another computer, such as a robot controller while Servo runs on a laptop, are measured as if they were Servo's clock, and Conduit cannot tell.
      • An empty Joint states field is saved as /joint_states. A namespaced topic, for example /arm/joint_states, must be in the field.
      • The host must run on wall-clock time. With simulation time (use_sim_time), HOLD cannot be used.

      HOLD is refused until the robot clock is known. The status line says why:

      Message Meaning
      "Reading the robot clock from Joint states" Conduit is still collecting stamps (it needs ten Joint states messages).
      "Robot and device clocks are more than a day apart (simulation time?). HOLD is off." The stamps are not wall-clock time: simulation time, unstamped messages, or a clock that was never set.
      "Set Joint states in the profile. HOLD needs the robot clock." The profile has no Joint states topic.

      If the robot clock becomes unusable while HOLD is held (a simulator starts, say), the arm lets go and shows "Robot clock changed. Release and press again."

    3. Gripper (optional). A control_msgs/action/GripperCommand server. The profile holds its action name (Gripper action) and, in its Gripper section, the open and closed positions in metres (Open (m), Closed (m); the Franka Panda's fingers travel from 0.0 to 0.04). With Gripper joint set to the finger joint in Joint states, the arm view shows the opening the robot reports.

    4. Named poses (optional). A move_group that publishes the SRDF on /robot_description_semantic and serves /move_action, and Servo's pause service. Conduit sends the moves itself, so there is nothing to install for them, and the profile needs nothing. See Named poses.

    When the arm view opens, Conduit calls the profile's Servo command type service (/servo_node/switch_command_type by default) to switch Servo to TWIST, and retries every few seconds while Servo is missing or refuses. The Servo chip shows the result: "Connecting to Servo", "Servo not found", "Servo refused TWIST", then TWIST, or what Servo reports ("Slowing: near singularity", "Halted: joint bound", …), and "Servo not responding" when no Servo status has arrived for a second. HOLD stays off until Servo is ready.

    The arm view

    The arm view runs the Device Pose sensor, which uses ARKit and needs the camera to itself, so opening it pauses the enabled sensors that use the camera (the cameras, except the wide back camera while LiDAR is on, whose images already come from ARKit) and Illuminance. When any of them is enabled, Conduit asks first ("Opening pauses … while the arm view is open. Device Pose uses ARKit, which needs the camera to itself. Continue?"). Closing the view restarts what it paused and stops Device Pose if the view started it.

    The wrist camera fills the screen behind the controls. Pinch it to scale between the whole image and filling the screen, and drag to pan where the image is larger than the screen. Each layout keeps its own zoom.

    On iPhone the layout follows how you hold the phone; with the rotation lock on, it stays as it is. Turning the phone releases HOLD and stops a gripper drag, and keeps the alignment. Closing the view returns the app to portrait.

    • Landscape, two hands. Along the top: the status chips and the status line, the pose menu and Align, then the gear and close. In the bottom corners, the gripper arc under one thumb and HOLD under the other. Without a Gripper action there is no arc, and HOLD sits in the bottom right corner. An iPad always uses this layout, in any orientation.
    • Upright, one hand. Along the top: the status chips, the gear and close; under them the pose menu and Align. The status line sits above the bottom controls. HOLD is in the bottom corner under the thumb of the hand that holds the phone, with the gripper arc around it.

    The status chips: Servo, motion tracking (only while it is not normal, for example "tracking limited (excessive motion)" or "no tracking"), the wrist camera's latency and frame rate ("42 ms · 30 fps") and, when the profile has a Diagnostics topic, the diagnostics. Tap the diagnostics chip to open the diagnostics; this releases HOLD, stops a gripper drag and cancels a pose move. The status line appears only when something needs saying: a fault, a refusal, why HOLD is off, a pose move, or the free-time notice. The camera's captions (STALE, "No data", "No wrist camera topic", …) and the warning that the profile expects other connection settings sit in the space between the controls.

    When the top row runs out of room (a smaller iPhone, larger text), the pose menu narrows (a long pose name is cut at its end) down to the width of "Poses", then shows only its symbol, and last Align shows only its symbol.

    The gear's panel holds the settings that change the motion:

    setting values what it does
    Max speed 0.5×, 1× (default), 2× Scales translation. At 1× the end effector moves at up to 0.25 m/s.
    Translation on (default) / off Off sends no translation, so the end effector turns without moving.
    Rotation on (default) / off Off sends no rotation. Rotation goes up to 1 rad/s.
    Gripper Left thumb (default) / Right thumb Landscape, with a Gripper action: which bottom corner has the gripper arc; HOLD takes the other.
    Hand Left hand / Right hand (default) Upright: the hand that holds the phone, which gets HOLD and the arc.

    Gripper and Hand are one choice: the hand that holds the phone upright is the one whose thumb has HOLD in landscape. Conduit keeps it per robot on the device. Max speed, Translation and Rotation start at their defaults each time the view opens. The panel is off while HOLD is held.

    Using it

    1. Align. Stand behind the robot's base and face the way its base frame's +x axis points, which is the robot's forward direction. Point the back camera that way, or tilt the device down to watch the screen, down to lying flat with the top of the screen pointing that way, and tap Align: that heading becomes the base frame's +x. If you face the robot instead, the motion is mirrored. Align says why when it refuses: "Tilt the device down: the camera points too far up" (the camera points more than 60° above the horizon), "Point the top of the screen toward the robot's front" or "Wait for motion tracking, then Align". Align again after motion tracking restarts or fails ("Motion tracking restarted. Align again.", "Motion tracking failed. Align again.").
    2. Hold HOLD and move. HOLD is dimmed until the link is up, Servo is ready, the robot clock is known and you have tapped Align. The status line says what it waits for ("Connecting…", a Servo problem, the robot clock, "Starting…"). A press when only Align is missing opens a "Tap Align first" dialog. A press also needs normal motion tracking, and is refused with "Waiting for motion tracking" or "Motion tracking is limited. Move the device slowly." without it. HOLD turns green and reads HOLDING while the arm follows.
    3. Release to stop. Releasing HOLD sends three zero twists at once. Tracking loss, a covered camera, a pose jump or a lost link also stop the arm, with one zero twist ("The pose jumped. Release and press again.", "Motion tracking paused. Release and press again."); release and press again to resume.
    4. Gripper. With HOLD released, tap the arc to open or close the gripper, or drag along it to set an opening. The readout along the arc says "40 % open" ("Moving · 40 % open" while a goal runs). The white handle is the opening asked for, and with a Gripper joint the track fills teal up to the opening the robot reports. Until the opening is known the readout says "Unknown: tap the arc": a drag sends nothing, and a tap opens. The gripper is off while HOLD is held ("Off while HOLD is held"), and a HOLD press while your thumb is on the gripper is refused with "Lift your thumb off the gripper first".

    Without Conduit Plus, the status line shows the weekly free time's warnings for a few seconds ("30 minutes of free time left this week", "5 minutes left — the session stops when this week's free time is used up"). When the free time is used up while you hold HOLD or a pose move runs, the arm keeps going until you release HOLD and the move ends, for at most 5 more minutes. Then Conduit stops the arm and shows the Conduit Plus paywall over the arm view; closing the paywall closes the arm view.

    VoiceOver: HOLD is the "Clutch" button: double-tap and hold to move the arm, and release to stop. The gripper is the adjustable "Gripper opening": double-tap to open or close it, swipe up or down to open or close it by 10 %, with Open and Close as actions. The wrist camera has "Show whole image" and "Fill screen" actions.

    Conduit also publishes the raw pose (<namespace>/pose, /tf) and <namespace>/teleop/clutch (std_msgs/Bool, true while the clutch is held, false on any pose discontinuity), so a custom consumer can move relative to the pose at engage time. The namespace is conduit unless you changed it in Settings. The pose and /tf come from the Device Pose sensor, which runs while the arm view is open.

    Named poses

    The pose menu moves the arm to a group state of the robot's SRDF, such as stand or home, through MoveIt. Conduit talks to move_group itself: a pick sends a moveit_msgs/action/MoveGroup goal to /move_action, and Conduit pauses MoveIt Servo for the move. The host needs no extra node and nothing to install for poses, and the robot profile has no pose settings. Named poses work on Jazzy and later.

    The pose menu

    The menu sits next to Align and lists every group state of the robot's arm group, in the order the SRDF has them and with the names exactly as the SRDF writes them (work, stand, ready, home, not "Work"). The pose the arm is at is checked.

    • The button reads the name of the pose the arm is at, or "Poses" when it is at none of them. At a pose it is teal with a checkmark. While the arm moves to a pose, it shows a spinner and the name of the pose you picked. A name longer than the button is cut at its end.
    • Picking a pose moves the arm there at once; there is no confirmation. Picking the pose the arm is already moving to does nothing. Picking another pose while the arm moves cancels that move and starts the new one once the cancel has ended.
    • The menu is off (the button is dimmed and does not open) while HOLD is pressed or held, while the link is down, after the session stopped or the view failed to set up, and for 1 s after the set of poses changes (the SRDF arrived late, or a changed SRDF dropped or reordered a pose). The pause keeps a tap meant for Align from moving the arm. VoiceOver says why: "Off while HOLD is held.", "Off while the robot link is down.", "Off: the session stopped.", "The pose list just changed.".
    • No menu appears without an SRDF that has states for the arm group. The menu appears as soon as the SRDF arrives. The Mac's view-only arm view has no menu.

    The arm group is the parent_group of the SRDF's end effector. When several end effectors name a parent group, the first whose group has states counts. Without an end effector, it is the first group that is not an end effector's own group. The gripper's own states (such as open and closed) are therefore not offered. The goals plan for that group. A state name that an SRDF repeats is listed once and moves to the last state of that name, as in MoveIt; two names that differ only in case are two poses.

    Conduit reads the SRDF from /robot_description_semantic and sends goals to /move_action, both absolute names. A move_group in a namespace publishes and serves them under that namespace, so the arm view finds no poses and shows no menu.

    What a pick does

    • The goal plans and executes a move of the arm group to the state's joint positions, read from the newest SRDF when the move starts: one joint constraint per joint of the state, at ±0.001 rad, from the arm's current state, with one planning attempt, 5 s planning time, and velocity and acceleration scaled to 0.2.
    • Servo is paused before the goal and resumed when the move ends, whether it succeeds, fails or is cancelled. Conduit uses Servo's std_srvs/srv/SetBool pause service next to the profile's Servo command type service: /servo_node/pause_servo by default. While the move runs, the Servo chip reads "Servo paused for the pose move". Without the pause service, the move still runs. When the arm view connects, Conduit also resumes Servo, in case an earlier session left it paused.
    • The status line reads "Moving to stand…" while the move runs. The checkmark comes from the profile's Joint states topic: the arm is at a pose when every joint of the state is within 0.01 rad (metres for a prismatic joint) of it. Joints compare modulo a full turn, so a joint exactly one turn away from the SRDF value also counts as at the pose.
    • HOLD stops a pose move. Pressing HOLD cancels the move ("Stopping the move to stand…"), and the press does not engage, even if you keep your finger down. Once the move has ended and Servo is resumed, usually well under a second later, the status line reads "Pose move stopped. Press HOLD again.": press HOLD again to move the arm. The arm stops where it is, so the button reads "Poses" until the arm reaches a pose.
    • The background, opening the diagnostics, closing the arm view and the free-time stop cancel the move too, and resume Servo.
    • 30 s after the pick a move that has not ended is cancelled. When move_group does not end a cancel within 5 s, the arm view stops waiting for it.

    Cancels need the link

    Conduit cancels a move by sending the cancel to move_group, which only works while the link is up. A move running when Wi-Fi drops completes on the host. After the reconnect, the status line reads "Waiting for the move to stand to end…": HOLD stays off and Servo stays paused until the joint states show the arm still for a second (counted from 6 s after the pick, while move_group may still be planning), or 30 s after the pick. Then Conduit resumes Servo.

    If the app is killed and opened again while its move still runs, the new arm view knows nothing about that move: it resumes Servo when it connects and allows HOLD while the arm may still be moving. Keep the arm's own emergency stop within reach.

    Host side

    1. Make move_group publish the SRDF. The move_group.launch.py and demo.launch.py that the MoveIt Setup Assistant generates do. A launch file that builds move_group from MoveItConfigsBuilder needs .planning_scene_monitor(publish_robot_description_semantic=True), or the parameter publish_robot_description_semantic: True on the move_group node; it is off by default in Jazzy. Check it with:

      ros2 param get /move_group publish_robot_description_semantic   # Boolean value is: True

    2. Check that move_group serves the MoveGroup action and that Servo has its pause service:

      ros2 action list | grep move_action           # /move_action
ros2 service type /servo_node/pause_servo     # std_srvs/srv/SetBool

    When a pose move fails

    The status line says what happened, until the next pick or HOLD press:

    Message What to check
    "move_group not found on /move_action" move_group is not running, or runs in a namespace.
    "move_group refused the move to stand" move_group rejected the goal; check the move_group log on the host.
    "move_group did not answer the move to stand" move_group did not accept the goal, or end a cancel, within 5 s: it hangs or restarted.
    "The move to stand failed: …" Planning or execution failed; the rest is MoveIt's error code (see below) and, when move_group sends one, its own message after "MoveIt:". Without either, the line reads "The move to stand failed": check the move_group log on the host.
    "move_group stopped the move to stand" move_group cancelled the goal itself, for example because another client sent it a goal ("…: preempted (-7)").
    "The move to stand took over 30 s and was stopped" The 30 s limit. A slow arm needs states closer together.
    "stand is no longer in the SRDF" A pick waited for a cancel while a new SRDF dropped that state.
    "stand has no joint values in the SRDF" The state names no joint with one number as its value.
    "The move to stand was not sent: …" Conduit could not send the goal, for example "Not connected to the robot".
    "Waiting for the move to stand to end…" The link dropped during the move (see above).

    The most useful MoveIt error codes, as the status line shows them after "The move to stand failed:":

    Text Code What to do
    "planning failed (-1)" PLANNING_FAILED No path to the state within 5 s. Move the arm closer with HOLD, or check obstacles in the planning scene.
    "the pose is in collision or outside the joint limits (-27)" GOAL_STATE_INVALID The group state itself is invalid. Fix it in the SRDF.
    "the arm starts in collision (-10)" START_STATE_IN_COLLISION The arm is in collision where it is. Jog it clear with HOLD, then pick again.
    "the controller failed (-4)" CONTROL_FAILED The trajectory controllers on the host refused or aborted the trajectory. Check that they are active (ros2 control list_controllers).
    "timed out (-6)" TIMED_OUT Execution took longer than the controllers allow. Check the host's controllers.
    "unknown planning group (-15)" INVALID_GROUP_NAME move_group has no group of the name the SRDF's arm group has: its MoveIt configuration is not the SRDF it publishes.

    Other codes read as their short text with the number, or "MoveIt error ".

    If the checkmark does not appear after a move, the arm stopped further than 0.01 rad from the state, or the Joint states topic does not carry every joint of the state (ros2 topic echo --once /joint_states --field name).

    Example: poses that Servo can move from

    Servo stops at a singularity ("Halted: singularity" on the Servo chip) and near the joint limits ("Halted: joint bound"). An arm's ready or home state can be singular, and then HOLD does not move the arm after a pick of it. On the six-joint desk arm below (the Maker Arm), both are: the Jacobian condition number at the gripper is 35.2 at ready and 42.2 at home, above Servo's hard_stop_singularity_threshold of 30. Give the arm group states away from singularities and joint limits, and list them first, so they are the first entries of the menu. On that arm, two such states, work and stand, sit before ready and home:

    <group_state name="work" group="arm">
    <joint name="link_002_joint" value="0"/>
    <joint name="link_003_joint" value="-0.95"/>
    <joint name="link_004_joint" value="1.2"/>
    <joint name="link_005_joint" value="-0.7"/>
    <joint name="link_006_joint" value="-0.1"/>
    <joint name="link_007_joint" value="0"/>
</group_state>
<group_state name="stand" group="arm">
    <joint name="link_002_joint" value="0"/>
    <joint name="link_003_joint" value="-1.6"/>
    <joint name="link_004_joint" value="1.6"/>
    <joint name="link_005_joint" value="-1.25"/>
    <joint name="link_006_joint" value="0"/>
    <joint name="link_007_joint" value="0"/>
</group_state>

    That arm's menu lists work, stand, ready and home, in that order. The condition number is 25.4 at work and 21.7 at stand: between Servo's lower_singularity_threshold (17) and the hard stop, so Servo moves along every axis and slows down near them. To check a state, pick it, wait for the checkmark, hold HOLD and move the device along each axis: the Servo chip should say TWIST or "Slowing: …", not "Halted: …". The values are the desk arm's, measured in simulation; find your arm's with the arm in place, its joints well inside their limits plus Servo's joint_limit_margins.

    iPad and Mac

    An iPad drives the arm like an iPhone, always in the two-handed layout. iPadOS pauses ARKit while Conduit shares the screen with other apps (Split View or a window); the tracking chip then reads "Camera paused: use Conduit full screen", and HOLD stops working until Conduit is full screen again. The diagnostics open in a sheet.

    A Mac has no ARKit world tracking, so its arm view is view-only: the wrist camera (pinch and drag work, and it opens on the whole image), the Servo status from the profile's Servo status topic, and the diagnostics, with "Arm control needs an iPhone or iPad. This view shows the wrist camera, Servo and diagnostics." There is no HOLD, gripper, Align or pose menu, and the view pauses no sensors and does not start Device Pose.

    Servo overlay

    conduit_teleop installs a MoveIt Servo parameter overlay at share/conduit_teleop/config/servo_overlay.yaml (source: ros/conduit_teleop/config/servo_overlay.yaml). Build conduit_teleop as described in Teleoperation to get it.

    /**:
  ros__parameters:
    moveit_servo:
      incoming_command_timeout: 0.5
      command_in_type: "speed_units"
    parameter value what it does
    moveit_servo.command_in_type speed_units Servo reads the twist as m/s and rad/s, which is what Conduit sends (not unitless −1 … 1 commands).
    moveit_servo.incoming_command_timeout 0.5 Servo drops a twist whose header.stamp is older than this on the host clock (default 0.1 s). Conduit stamps its twists on the robot's clock and they cross Wi-Fi, so 0.1 s drops many of them; 0.5 s keeps them, and a lost link stops the arm within about half a second (see Time sync above). Releasing the clutch stops the arm at once with explicit zero twists.

    The /** key applies the file to any node, so it works whatever your launch file names the Servo node.

    Load it after your robot's own Servo parameters. A node applies its parameter sources in order, so the last one wins:

    from launch.substitutions import PathJoinSubstitution
from launch_ros.actions import Node
from launch_ros.substitutions import FindPackageShare

servo_node = Node(
    package="moveit_servo", executable="servo_node", name="servo_node",
    parameters=[servo_params, robot_description, robot_description_semantic,
                robot_description_kinematics,
                PathJoinSubstitution([FindPackageShare("conduit_teleop"), "config", "servo_overlay.yaml"])],
)

    Check that it took effect (with your Servo node's name):

    ros2 param get /servo_node moveit_servo.incoming_command_timeout   # Double value is: 0.5
ros2 param get /servo_node moveit_servo.command_in_type            # String value is: speed_units
